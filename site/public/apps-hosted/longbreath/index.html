How to do it

Before any breath-hold training

Breath-holds starve the brain of oxygen gradually, and you can black out without feeling it coming. On land, sitting or lying down, that is almost always harmless: your body starts breathing again by itself. In water, it can be fatal within minutes. So these rules are not optional:

  • Dry land only. Never do these tables in a pool, bath, lake or sea, and never alone near water.
  • Sit or lie down somewhere you can't fall. Never while driving, standing, or walking.
  • Never hyperventilate (fast, deep breathing) before a hold. It hides the warning signal and makes blackout more likely.
  • Stop if you feel tingling, tunnel vision, confusion, chest pain or strong dizziness.
  • Check with a doctor first if you have a heart or blood pressure condition, epilepsy, are pregnant, or have any lung condition.
  • If you later want to train in water, do it only in a certified freediving course with an instructor watching you.

Measure your pulse

Find your pulse on the thumb side of your wrist with two fingers. Press start, then tap the button once for every beat you feel for 30 seconds.
